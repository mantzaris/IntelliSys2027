\section{Status and Research Question}
\textbf{Working research draft; not ready for submission.}
\input{generated/status.tex}
The human author's scientific review is pending. The title is provisional until
the findings justify a final contribution.

We ask how duplication, partial overlap, and small consequential differences in
delegated tasks and reports affect decision quality. One possible failure is
unnecessary sensitivity to repeated information. Another is failure to respond to
similar information whose scope or value changes a mandatory constraint. Neither
behavior follows simply from report similarity: two messages can use the same
words about different resources, while different words can express the same claim
from the same source.

The candidate contribution is a reproducible, controlled test of complementary
constraint integration and preservation of scope differences across restricted
summary boundaries. We do not propose evidence double counting, provenance,
paraphrase sensitivity, or hierarchical communication as new discoveries.
Conclusions must remain specific to evaluated models, tasks, interfaces, and
resource conditions; deployment prevalence and general intelligence are outside
the study's scope.

\section{Related Work and Remaining Distinction}
Gao et al.~\cite{copies} are the closest comparison. Their controlled sensing,
web-document, and agent-written-log experiments measure how restatements affect
beliefs and decisions. Their hierarchy and summary conditions already address
communication structure. In their agent-team testbed, the aggregator receives the
full log. Our proposed distinction is a conjunction of scoped requirements passed
through managers whose root receives summaries without the raw reports. A generic
finding that repeated evidence shifts an answer would not establish this distinction.

Bara~\cite{sybil} separates ancestry from report similarity and models correlated
extraction errors. We therefore distinguish additional verification executions
from new evidence sources and do not infer independence from fresh model calls.
HiddenBench~\cite{hidden} examines information distributed among agents and failures
to elicit unshared facts. Our frozen replay supplies the complete required report
set before aggregation, allowing delivery failures to be separated from later
summary loss. MAST~\cite{mast} provides a broad taxonomy of multi-agent failure;
our deterministic, paired interventions address a narrower mechanism.

The current scaling study's v3~\cite{scaling} examines architecture--task alignment.
We cite that version without importing numerical claims from earlier versions.
Hi-Agreement~\cite{distributed} investigates honest and deceptive testimony in
distributed fact recovery, motivating separate false-assertion controls here.
D3MAS~\cite{d3mas} is relevant to hierarchical knowledge organization, but its
arXiv page carries an author-withdrawal notice concerning methodology; we do not
rely on its reported performance.

Behavioral invariance and directional testing are established by
CheckList~\cite{checklist}. PAWS~\cite{paws} illustrates the distinction between
surface overlap and semantic equivalence. Metadata-sensitive conflict
resolution~\cite{metadata} motivates explicit version-authority rules, while
position effects in long contexts~\cite{middle} motivate length and ordering
controls. These alternatives must be considered before attributing failures to
duplication itself.

\section{Benchmark and Executable Truth}
\subsection{Candidate Schedule Selection}
Each primary instance contains jobs with positive integer durations, nonnegative
resource demands, resource capacities, an acyclic precedence graph, and candidate
start times. Optional releases, deadlines and downtime are supported. Jobs execute
non-preemptively on half-open intervals $[s_j,s_j+d_j)$. For each resource $r$,
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{j:s_j\leq t<s_j+d_j} q_{jr}\leq C_r
\end{equation}
must hold at every event interval. Precedence requires $s_u+d_u\leq s_v$ for every
edge $(u,v)$. Releases and deadlines bound starts and completions. A positive
resource demand may not overlap that resource's downtime.

Let $F(c)$ be the conjunction of all mandatory predicates for candidate $c$,
and $M(c)=\max_j(s_j+d_j)$. The answer minimizes $M(c)$ over supplied candidates
with $F(c)=1$. A public priority ordering resolves equal objectives; NONE is correct
when the feasible set is empty. This is candidate selection, not unrestricted
optimal scheduling. The event-sweep verifier is cross-checked against a separately
coded integer-time occupancy checker. Tests also cover exact interval boundaries,
deadline equality, invalid inputs, ties and NONE.

\subsection{Paired Changes and Secondary Workflow}
Original and modified worlds share candidate identifiers and controlled structure.
Every pair records changed fields, mutation preconditions, affected predicates,
oracle intermediate results, and both correct answers. Decision-changing and
decision-preserving pairs are labeled by executable outcomes rather than intent.
Scheduling currently implements capacity, resource identity and precedence edits.
A separate finite Boolean workflow implements quantifiers, conditions and
explicit version selection. The latter is reported as a secondary task family,
not as scheduling evidence.

Generation randomizes identifiers, report order, candidate ordering and tie
priority without consulting a model. Development and validation differ in parameter
ranges; evaluation additionally reserves fork--join structure and rendering forms.
All variants of one base problem remain in the same split. The construction is
deliberately small and controlled; its limited graph and constraint diversity
restricts external validity.

\subsection{Scope, Truth and Ancestry}
A typed claim carries task, candidate, entity, predicate, value, units,
applicability, version, source identifiers, parent reports, verification execution
and operation type. Scope equivalence excludes ancestry and asserted value, so
compatible claims can still conflict. Copies preserve the source and execution;
fresh verification can have a distinct execution while retaining the same source.

The task dependency graph, organizational hierarchy, communication/access graph,
and report/evidence ancestry graph are stored separately. Scheduling job precedence
is an additional graph, not the graph of verification assignments. Evaluator-only
mutation and correctness labels are kept outside model prompts. Shared-state action
races and destructive concurrent tool effects are excluded.

\section{Replay and Aggregation Protocol}
\subsection{Reports and Organization}
Template reports are frozen from typed assertions before inference. The corpus
contains no model-generated paraphrases. Code review and integrity tests check
claim preservation; human linguistic review remains an open review item. The
repetition conditions show a selected report once, four times exactly, or once
with three templated paraphrases. No required finding is removed. A prespecified
rotation chooses the repeated candidate/task, preferring an unchanged report
across worlds. Version changes may require a declared exception.

The flat organization uses one root call with the complete report set. The
hierarchy uses two managers with frozen partitions and one root; copies initially
remain in the original branch. The root receives the summaries and a value-free
registry of required claim scopes. The registry is available to every policy,
including ordinary synthesis, and must be considered part of the evaluated
interface. It does not reveal which assertions are true.

\subsection{Policies and Observable Outputs}
Ordinary synthesis states the same feasibility and optimization rule as the
checklist and scope-aware policies. The checklist asks for required-task coverage.
Scope-aware instructions additionally preserve entities, conditions, versions,
unique findings and unresolved conflicts while consolidating equivalent ancestry.
A challenge is not an automatic veto; a timestamp alone does not authorize a
correction. The proposed policy has no privileged truth labels.

Final outputs contain a choice, class probabilities, explicit supporting
assertions and unresolved scope keys. Intermediate outputs use the same assertion
contract without a final choice. We score emitted claims, not private reasoning.
The initial development interface required strict JSON but frequently produced
Markdown fences and incorrect field types. Those outputs remain failures under
that protocol. A second development interface uses named assertion fields and a
common JSON schema decoder for all policies. This is an interface revision, not a
repair that retrospectively changes the initial run's primary scores.
Subsequent development revisions make scope identifiers descriptive, map objective
labels explicitly to makespan or cost, and elicit a short feasibility/objective
assessment for each candidate before the final choice. These assessments are model
outputs, not oracle values. This common scaffold narrows the difference between
ordinary synthesis and the checklist. A role-specific schema constrains managers'
choice to null and their final-assessment fields to empty collections, enforcing
their summary-only contract. The failed earlier manager outputs remain recorded. Predicate criteria are
recorded separately from applicability: a failed resource check still applies.
A tokenizer audit also found an extra beginning-of-sequence token in historical
Nemo calls. Those checks characterize their original interface, not clean model
rankings. Subsequent inference submits native token IDs directly and freezes
dates inserted by chat templates.

\section{Execution Integrity and Analysis}
Model revisions, tokenizer revisions, chat-template hashes, precision, dependency
versions, sampling settings and output limits are recorded. Inference uses only
the allocated NVIDIA RTX 6000 Ada GPU. No paid model API is used. Raw requests and
responses are immutable; accepted calls and base-cluster completions are journaled.
Cache keys include stochastic repetition identifiers. Resume reuses completed
malformed outputs instead of sampling until valid. Interrupted, unaccepted calls
are identifiable and are not silently treated as completed failures or successes.
Local result mirrors prevent the pod from being the sole copy of completed data.

Let $E_{p,a,r}$ denote decision error averaged within base pair $p$, organization
$a$ and repetition condition $r$. Repetition harm is
\begin{equation}
 H_{a,r}=\mathbb{E}_p[E_{p,a,r}-E_{p,a,\mathrm{single}}].
\end{equation}
The hierarchy interaction is $H_{\mathrm{hierarchical},r}-H_{\mathrm{flat},r}$.
A higher overall hierarchical error rate is not itself amplification of repetition.
Correct responsiveness and correct stability require both paired answers to be
correct, respectively on changing and preserving pairs. Merely switching or
retaining a choice is insufficient.

The initial analysis manifest defines exact-copy and paraphrase effects (H1),
the difference in pair-failure effects between consequential and harmless edits
(H2), the hierarchy interaction (H3), and scope-versus-checklist error and retention
contrasts (H4). Analysis resamples complete base clusters, stratified by mutation
family and consequence, retaining dependence across worlds, arms, models and
stochastic repetitions. Model-specific results are mandatory; a pooled estimate
uses equal model weights. Scheduling and workflow estimates remain separate.
No confirmatory significance claim is based on the development runs.
Collapsed empirical bootstrap intervals are flagged: no observed variation in a
small sample does not establish zero population effect or justify a power claim.

Malformed completed trajectories count as operational failures in primary
accuracy. Calibration uses valid complete probability vectors and reports their
availability fraction. Additional outcomes include invalid selections, assignment
and delivery coverage, summary retention, scope transfer, copied-claim consolidation,
false-assertion adoption and resource use. Input/output tokens and elapsed batch
time are measured. Allocating batch time across requests is a cost-accounting
convention, not a direct measurement of per-request GPU kernel time.
Equal integer and integral-float objective values count as equivalent numeric
claims; Boolean truth remains distinct from numeric one. Claim-score corrections
are recomputed from immutable responses with a versioned scorer. An incompatible
scope-citation metric records assertions supported by multiple reports whose
scopes do not all bind that assertion; this is an emitted-attribution measure.
False-assertion emission is separated from an uncontested false finding: the latter
requires the false value, absence of the contrary true value, and no unresolved
flag for that scope. Neither establishes a hidden belief or causal reasoning path.

\section{Supporting Design and Threats to Validity}
The supporting design contrasts overlapping and less-overlapping presentations
with identical distinct claims, exact removal versus similarity removal,
scope-aware consolidation, branch placement, raw-report bypass, and global
reviewers matched to the hierarchy's call count. Neutral material controls for
length; ancestry withholding and corrupted relationships test provenance use.
Instruction-only controls separate metadata from additional prompting. Distinct
information, ordering and report slots are held fixed where possible, with residual
token differences recorded. The result sections explicitly identify execution status.
Placement of distinct scoped reports and placement of exact copies are separate
contrasts. Moving a report changes branch load, which is recorded as a residual
difference. Similarity removal currently uses character overlap, so its findings
cannot be generalized to learned semantic deduplication.

Corrupted reports require visible authoritative evidence or an allowed
insufficient-information answer. False favorable assertions and false objections
must be separated. Live workers receive role-specific task data without peer
reports; four essential assignments remain in principal comparisons. Redundant
fresh execution differs from frozen copying. Fixed output allowances alone do not
match total computation, and coverage displacement requires its own label.

Major validity threats include small synthetic task diversity, structured scope
registries that assist all policies, template-based language, restricted model
families, formatting constraints, limited stochastic repetitions, and output caps.
Shared weight families and shared source inputs can induce dependent errors.
Retention associations alone cannot establish faithful internal reasoning or
causal mediation. Summary bypass offers a direct intervention, but its additional
input and compute must also be reported. Null effects with wide intervals are
inconclusive; beneficial hierarchy or sufficient simple checklists must be reported
with the same prominence as harmful effects.

\section{Conclusion}
The implemented benchmark makes scope, ancestry, coverage and candidate feasibility
explicit and auditable. Current development measurements constrain interpretation:
interface validity and ordinary task competence must be established before the
proposed duplication comparisons can support a paper. The contribution and final
title remain conditional on the completed experiments and human scientific review.
