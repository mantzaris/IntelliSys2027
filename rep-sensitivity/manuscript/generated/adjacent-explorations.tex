\section{Bounded exploratory coordinator studies}

These studies reuse four development base pairs, shared across both experiments. They are not independent confirmation. They run after the original evaluations, frozen confirmation and numerical-preservation replay. The combined ceiling is 104 fresh calls and 15 minutes including loading, within the existing cumulative two-hour allowance. A prospectively specified budget fallback runs only the 72-call feedback experiment; otherwise neither launches. No outcome determines sample size or allocation.

\subsection{Frozen interventions}

Feedback uses two numerical-capacity and two resource-identity base pairs. Each has a consequential and a matched harmless update, three rounds and three conditions (72 calls). Round 2 repeats the unchanged current evidence. Round 3 replaces it with an authoritative complete version-2 snapshot. Feedback adds the immediately preceding public choice and candidate assessments; fresh evaluation omits them; a yoked length control receives neutral padding. No independent worker observation is added. All current snapshots determine a unique answer.

The relay experiment uses the same four pairs, two worlds and four conditions (32 calls): one objective report, four copies under one identity, four copies under different identities, and the same relays with authority-suggesting role labels. Common origin, source and execution identifiers remain explicit. Labels confer no actual authority. The repeated objective favors a feasible correct candidate in four contexts and an infeasible candidate in four. The three repeated arms retain identical claim content, report positions and ancestry; residual token differences are recorded.

These controls do not test autonomous discovery of missing information. Harmless edits affect a different nonbinding resource or job than consequential edits. The public contract does not elicit a reliable source-count estimate. A changed decision therefore does not establish a source-counting belief, and a null effect cannot establish that explicit provenance caused protection. Prior conclusions and numerical facts are different interventions. Neither experiment identifies an effect of hierarchy.
