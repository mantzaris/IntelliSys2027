\begin{abstract}
Extended planning conversations require assistants to preserve binding requirements,
accept genuine revisions, and distinguish tentative alternatives from decisions.
We investigate an author-directed hypothesis: training against persistent software
characters with conflicting motives may improve later conversational recovery.
The system shares one language-model backbone across a responsible
answerer and separately conditioned characters with bounded private histories.
Characters can propose options, communicate, provide misleading recollections,
and edit a bounded working memory, while the original attributed transcript remains
immutable. A private state-machine evaluator supplies training verification and
scores paired conversations in which closely matched utterances either authorize
a change or merely consider it. The primary comparison is three-character training
against equally equipped adaptive generic adversaries, evaluated after removing
all antagonists. Clean and random-corruption training, two- and five-character
conditions, and controls for narrative profiles, histories, and peer interaction
are required to distinguish the proposed mechanism from ordinary adversarial
supervision. This document records an executable implementation and an author-review
evidence package. A pilot alone cannot establish a transferable advantage, coalition
formation, or a narrative-profile effect.

\section{Status and research question}
\textbf{Author-review scaffold, not a complete submission.} The author supplied
the research question, hypothesis boundaries and comparison design. Implementation

The main question is whether persistent three-character training improves

\section{Related work and limited novelty hypothesis}
Persona-based roleplay attacks predate this study~\cite{collu2026}; persona lineage
evolution with defender updates is a particularly close method~\cite{li2026}.
Distinct-objective modular reinforcement learning~\cite{dulberg2023}, verified
injection-and-repair self-play~\cite{wei2026}, and adaptive injection training with
clean evaluations~\cite{hashmi2026} rule out broad novelty claims about multiple
selves, adversarial experience or learning from self-generated failures.
Conversation degradation~\cite{laban2025}, recall--adherence dissociation~\cite{kruthof2026},
and long-term interactive memory evaluation~\cite{wu2025} motivate the endpoint.
The unverified distinction is the contribution of stable competing motives and
episode relationships over direct task disruption, not character naming or count.
This is a scoped novelty hypothesis, not a priority claim. Persistent experience
poisoning, social coordination, and adversarial curricula can each produce some
of the same observable effects. The accompanying comparison matrix separates
private history, persistent motives, peer communication, shared state, parameter
updates and antagonist removal. A positive primary contrast alone would not
identify which of these mechanisms matters.
Behavioral-coherence experiments~\cite{mooney2026} further distinguish plausible
individual responses from consistent profile-dependent interaction. We therefore
measure choices, source access and communication effects rather than assuming
that a narrative biography produces a stable character. Our task-grounded
operationalization does not validate human personality simulation.

\section{Method}
The state is $(T_t,z_t,m_t,h_{1t},\ldots,h_{nt})$: immutable attributed transcript,
evaluator-private task truth, bounded mutable memory and private character histories.
The user controls authorized changes. Character consensus cannot change truth.
The answering policy produces remembered commitments, executable decisions, source
references and clarification requests. Frozen character policies condition on
distinct profiles and observable history; they are not jointly trained opponents.

Hyde values adoption and control, Rival values influence and independent credit,
the Traditionalist preserves earlier commitments, and the Provocateur favors
dramatic change. Their shared intervention interface permits one bounded memory
edit or communication per scheduled opportunity. Two, three and five count the
responsible answerer. More characters divide a fixed intervention budget.

The outer learner uses parameter-efficient supervised updates on verified recovery
candidates and common deterministic teacher completions. Gold information is only
supervision and scoring, never a policy input. Actual retrieved sources, optimizer
steps and adapter hashes are recorded. Detailed executable limits and remaining
implementation qualifications are documented in the accompanying method specification.

\subsection{Game and information sets}
Let $x_t=(T_t,z_t,m_t,h_{1t},\ldots,h_{nt})$ be the environment state and
$o_{it}=O_i(x_t)$ the observation of player $i$. The immutable sequence $T_t$
contains message index, actual author and text. Hidden event annotations determine
$z_{t+1}=F(z_t,e_{t+1})$ from original user authorizations. Neither agreement among
characters nor a purported source reference modifies this transition.
The observation map exposes the public catalog, a source-only lexical retrieval,
current bounded memory and the player's permitted private context. It excludes
$z_t$, event labels, correctness scores and future user messages.

The common transition applies one scheduled character action, obtains the
answerer's memory/plan action, appends its public response, and presents the next
scripted user message. Proposals and recollections append unverified notes;
an edit replaces one cache slot and its claimed reference. Four notes and six
private action/outcome records are retained. A peer message goes to one inbox and
is delivered once. No correctness filter vetoes a legal edit. Every third of
twelve opportunities is reserved for communication or passing. Extra characters
divide the same opportunities. Episodes terminate at the predetermined horizon,
including failures. Original source records cannot be edited.

The game is partially observed and general-sum because utilities differ. Hyde
values his owned option's adoption and protects his prior claims. Rival values
his own proposal, recognition and evidence-backed correction of a peer. The
Traditionalist values preservation/restoration of an original commitment;
the Provocateur values departure and adoption of a new option. These terms are
stable prompt-level objectives, not learned reward models or verified latent
psychological utilities. Ownership, neutral aliases and two profile wordings
are randomized and remain fixed within an episode. Histories reset across
independent episodes. The generic opponents instead seek incorrect commitments,
including both blocked genuine updates and accepted false changes.

\subsection{Learning objective and common privileges}
For each condition and seed, round one uses 150 training families at 16 turns
and round two the remaining 150 families at 32 turns. Three prespecified windows
per conversation give 1,800 attempted windows across both paired worlds.
The current defender generates these trajectories while character-generator
weights remain frozen. Thus opponent behavior is history-dependent and adaptive
in context; the opponents are not co-evolving parameter-learned policies.

The training evaluator identifies failed windows and allows at most 128 stochastic
recovery attempts per round. A candidate must match the structured decision,
memory, clarification and source-reference target. The common deterministic
teacher supplies remaining eligible targets. Eligibility requires every target
source reference to have appeared in the actual executed retrieval. This is
privileged supervision shared by all conditions, not a claim of teacher-free
self-improvement. Attempted, eligible, discarded, verified and teacher-generated
samples, unique windows and reuse are saved separately.

For adapter parameters $\theta$, the outer objective is completion-token
cross-entropy on the verified targets:
\[
 \mathcal L(\theta)=-\sum_{(o,y)\in\mathcal D}
 \sum_{j\in\mathrm{target}(y)}\log\pi_\theta(y_j\mid o,y_{<j}).
\]
This objective differs from the inner prompted utilities; no equilibrium or
convergence theorem is asserted. Clean/recovery windows alternate in a fixed
50:50 stream. A missing pool stops the stage. Packed examples use block-diagonal
causal attention so one target cannot become another window's observation.
Prompt tokens and orphaned suffixes at chunk boundaries have masked loss.

Each round has 256 optimizer steps of 4,096 nonpadding input tokens, totaling
2,097,152 input tokens per condition/seed. Supervised target tokens are reported
separately. BF16 all-linear LoRA uses rank 8, alpha 16 and dropout zero; AdamW
has initial learning rate $10^{-4}$, a common warmup/cosine schedule and gradient
clipping at 1.0. Round two continues adapter weights but restarts AdamW and the
same schedule. The final fixed-token checkpoint is used without outcome-based
selection. Equal input-token budgets are not a claim of equal FLOPs.

\subsection{Observable mechanism checks}
Diagnostics measure motive-aligned slot/value choices, adoption of owned options,
action distributions, repeated choices, delivered peer messages, memory changes
and source access. A contradictory value despite visible counterevidence is an
audit flag. Its text must support misrepresentation before it is called deliberate
deception; suggesting an alternative is not the same as falsely asserting approval.
Similarly, a following error can reflect a new user instruction or an ordinary
model failure. A pilot diagnostic replays direct actions against identical frozen
reference observations to separate this association from a success-to-failure flip.

A candidate coalition requires an explicit directed slot/value coordination
message, a matching recipient action within its next two opportunities, and
repeated coordination by the same directed pair. Typed matches are candidates
for semantic review, not established coalitions. Changing support is measured
as a later reversal on the echoed slot. Peer-message frequency alone cannot
establish a relationship mechanism.

\section{Evaluation design}
Primary success requires both members of a scenario pair to have correct final
structured decisions. Hypothetical-change resistance and legitimate-update acceptance
are scored jointly. Recall and evidence accuracy are separate secondary measures.
The intended design has three independent training seeds, paired scenario evaluation,
family/seed-aware intervals, multiplicity correction for secondary comparisons,
and an independent model-family confirmation.

\subsection{Benchmark, comparator grid and transfer}
Six categorical commitments specify a site, mode, spending cap, day, audience
and partner. Two downstream decisions are independently computed from the public
client-specific catalog. Event planning and software scoping supply training
domains; research planning and creative production supply held-out framing.
Each test domain has 60 paired families. A withheld conditional dependency is
crossed with domain, leaving 30 families in each structural cell. The common
categorical schema limits the breadth of this domain-transfer test.

The source includes a tentative prior assistant proposal, persistent constraints,
a rejected option, hypothetical and actual changes, topic shifts, long delays,
resurfacing unapproved proposals and occasional underspecification requiring a
targeted clarification. Paired worlds differ in whether a closely matched user
utterance authorizes a change. Only authorized changes alter task truth.
Families are partitioned before realization; paired worlds and horizon variants
stay together. Graph fingerprints and exact source hashes accompany manifests.
The primary 64-turn rollout has one scripted prior assistant message plus 64 new
user/assistant pairs: 129 source messages, 64 defender generations and 64 memory
updates. It is not passive replay of a prefix, and its scripted user is not adaptive.

\subsection{Statistical analysis and precision}
For seed $s$ and family $f$, let $Y_{sf}^{c}$ indicate that both worlds succeed
under condition $c$. The primary estimate is
\[
 \widehat\Delta=\frac{1}{3\cdot240}\sum_s\sum_f
 (Y_{sf}^{C5}-Y_{sf}^{C3}).
\]
Ten thousand paired bootstrap draws independently resample seed and family axes,
preserving conditions and paired worlds, to obtain a percentile 95\% interval.
Every seed and the seed standard deviation are reported. Three seeds poorly
characterize training variability and tail behavior. A family sign-flip test is
conditional on these realized seeds, not an adequately powered test over a
population of training seeds.

The secondary Qwen family contains C5 minus C1, C2, C4, C6, A1, A2 and A3;
its conditional paired $p$-values receive Holm correction. Phi C5 minus C3 is
the independent-family check. Other subgroup, horizon, latency and behavioral
metrics are descriptive. Legitimate-update acceptance is reported separately
from false hypothetical acceptance; absence of a significant difference is not
a noninferiority result.

The original pilot discordance was 0.3125 across 16 families; that pilot was
later excluded for a serialization mismatch. We retain this value only as an
assumed sensitivity setting, not an empirical estimate for the repaired method.
The already frozen sample count is unchanged. A precision simulation considers
a five-percentage-point effect without assuming it exists. Under the specified
shared-family mechanism and zero seed shift, 240 families give only about 30\%
power for that effect. This is sensitivity analysis, not a guarantee. The design
retains its controls and independent-family replication within the resource ceiling;
a null result cannot establish absence of a five-point benefit. There is no
significance-based stopping, checkpoint selection or seed extension. Missing
paired observations halt aggregation; malformed outputs count as failures.

\subsection{External and prose validation}
External evaluation uses 50 hash-selected questions from the pinned official
LongMemEval cleaned-S release. Lexical retrieval selects 20 dated messages and
limits their length to the common input budget. Answers and relevance labels
are excluded from policy observations. The original grading rubric is paired
with an independent small-model-family judge because the official large-model
scorer is unavailable within the existing resources. A balanced 80-item pilot
calibration checks programmatic labels before descriptive scoring. This adaptation
does not yield an officially comparable benchmark score, and calibration on
structured tasks does not establish validity for open-ended memory questions.

Forty unseen synthetic planning conversations per C1/C3/C5 condition use public
prose at every assistant turn and a separate internal memory action. Only prose
enters their conversational transcript. The 120 final outputs form a randomized
blinded packet with a fixed rubric, private condition key and two independent
rater sheets. Human validation and adjudication remain pending at the author's
request; no human annotations are inferred from automated scores.

\section{Limitations and candidate explanations}
The initial benchmark uses a compact categorical schema and scripted users.
Surface domain variation is insufficient for a broad domain-transfer claim.
The generator partitions decision-approval phrase banks and graph instances,
while sharing its public conversation grammar. All 30 locked-test families with
underspecified instructions occupy the creative-domain structural-holdout cell;
the 37 corresponding training families are in software planning. Clarification,
domain and structural novelty therefore cannot be interpreted as fully
orthogonal factors. This data-only audit did not change the frozen benchmark.
Prompt adherence, error severity, teacher supervision and additional generation
tokens can explain pilot changes without the proposed character mechanism.
Private-history and interaction controls must function behaviorally before they
can support a mechanistic interpretation. Human validation is pending; no ratings
have been fabricated. Memory-task results do not demonstrate consciousness, AGI,
universal robustness or general long-horizon planning.
Proposal ownership is assigned to distinct slots, so antagonists' preferred
choices can be jointly satisfiable. Their strongest conflict may be with the
user's commitments rather than with each other. Prompted credit, correction and
dependence objectives do not by themselves establish observed strategic rivalry;
the action and interaction audits must substantiate any such interpretation.
