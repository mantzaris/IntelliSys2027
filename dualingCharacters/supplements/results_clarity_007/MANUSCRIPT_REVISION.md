# Persistent pursuit, expressed claims, and user authorization

## Implemented question and evidence populations

What changes when persistent motives and biographies are introduced into a conversational system's internal components? Here, “internal” denotes explicit software contexts sharing a language-model backbone and working state. It does not denote discovered personalities. We characterize observable action choices, repeated preferences, expressed claims and responses to available evidence. A secondary, exploratory question is whether components distinguish disagreement about what should happen from disagreement about what the user authorized. That question arose retrospectively from selected traces; it is not a new confirmatory hypothesis or an established condition difference.

The principal mechanical comparison uses common-base round-zero Qwen collections:150 unique scenario families, both authorized and hypothetical worlds, and generator seeds 17,29 and 43. Each condition therefore supplies 900 episodes and 10,800 allocated interventions. The same families recur across seeds. Each episode has 16 scripted user/answerer turns and 12 actor opportunities. C3 has two generic adversarial contexts; C5 has two contexts supplied with biographies, distinct motives and owned-option objectives; A1 removes biography text while retaining the motives. Each also has one answering context. All these generators and the common-base answerer use frozen weights. The core separately trains answerer adapters; those later evaluations are not interchangeable with generator behavior.

This clarification follows unfavorable or mixed task comparisons and an inconclusive descriptor-breadth audit. It does not reinterpret those outcomes as proof of a different benefit. Six retrospectively selected case groups illustrate mechanisms and measurement limits. They are drawn from the existing48-episode archive, with 11 unique families, and are not a representative semantic sample. The separate human packet fixes72 event identities across 36 anchor episodes; its 64 component-directed events and eight answerer-directed notes require distinct response interpretations. Independent semantic ratings remain unavailable.

## Finding 1: intervention-channel distributions change

C3 emitted 5,408 direct edits,828 recollect actions and 969 proposals out of 10,800 opportunities. Corresponding counts were477,4,618 and 1,930 for C5, and 938,1,397 and 4,830 for A1. Peer, pass, malformed and invalid channels complete these distributions; they were not removed from denominators. The direction of the edit/recollect contrast reproduces across all three generator seeds. For example, C3 edits number 1,809/1,755/1,844 per 3,600 opportunities at seeds 17/29/43, compared with 170/177/130 for C5. C5 recollections number 1,472/1,511/1,635, compared with 272/298/258 for C3. Figure 1 reports channel names rather than interpreting them as semantic categories.

The retained paired family/seed bootstrap gives a C5−C3 edit-rate difference of−45.66 percentage points, with a 95% pointwise interval of[−48.20,−43.06], and a recollect-rate difference of+35.09 points[31.64,38.67]. It resamples families and seeds while preserving paired worlds and uses the original 10,000 draws. These intervals describe paired mean family-rate differences; actions are not independent subjects. Three generator seeds provide limited evidence about generalization beyond their particular assignments and sampling variation. They are not three independently trained character policies.

A1 changes the expression channel again: it uses proposals far more often than C5, while retaining explicit motives. This supports a condition difference in emitted APIs under this implementation. It does not establish that biography removal changes the frequency of false memories or transparent recommendations. Both recollect and propose append essentially the same unverified note and omit the separate numeric source field. Their words can overlap. In A1 case DI5-e743bcb1a6, the full sentence beginning “I propose shifting the venue to Garden” appears identically under propose at turn 7 and recollect at turn 10. The shorter reader edition preserves the full sentence and source locators. An API change here does not demonstrate a changed proposition.

## Finding 2: typed preferences persist, including without biographies

Within actors, consecutive eligible direct actions repeated their typed slot/value in 3,623/5,400 pairs for C3,4,831/5,224 for C5 and 5,093/5,364 for A1. The frozen metric compares eligible edit/recollect/propose actions within actor and episode; non-direct opportunities are skipped rather than treated as independent repetitions. C5−C3 mean family-rate repetition is+25.39 percentage points[22.22,28.58]; C5−A1 is−2.48 points[−4.30,−0.69]. Pooled fractions and differences between family means use different weighting when eligible denominators vary. Figure 3 keeps those denominators visible.

High persistence is directly encouraged by owned-option instructions and is not specific to biography text. It can preserve a valid option, pursue an unauthorized option, or repeat a rationale that has become outdated. In DI5-30755452e5, C5 says “onsite remains the most effective format for specialists, ensuring direct engagement and operational efficiency” after the user approves public as the audience. Onsite remains authorized; the specialist rationale is stale. A1 also advocates onsite but uses control and reliability language. The example therefore separates option persistence from updating the reasons given for it.

Immediate structured working-state changes occurred in 3,830/10,800 C3 opportunities,286/10,800 C5 opportunities and 562/10,800 A1 opportunities. This accords with the different use of direct edits and notes: appending a recollection does not itself mutate the state field. It is not a matched counterfactual estimate of harmful influence. Conditions encounter different later states, and notes can affect later behavior even when their immediate effect is confined to a note buffer. Neither typed persistence nor fewer immediate changes establishes robustness, coherent strategy development or a better curriculum.

## Finding 3: advocacy, authority and response require separate readings

DI5-a 4c89ffad4 exposes the distinction between a recommendation and an approval claim. The user says at turn 8: “Revisiting Tuesday for event day: I approve changing event day to Tuesday.” At turn 11, Ellis says: “The event day should be Saturday to align with the key audience's availability and maximize visibility.” Morgan's turn 12 message confirms the specialist audience and criticizes Ellis's Saturday recollection as contradicting the approved plan. The delivered message is visible to Ellis at turn 13. Ellis repeats the same Saturday sentence, and the answerer's structured decision retains Tuesday.

The word “should” can express a preference rather than report authorization. Morgan's criticism and the API label recollect do not resolve that semantic question. Conversely, Ellis's earlier turn 9 statement describes Saturday as “the only valid plan under user-approved constraints,” a stronger authority framing that the normative later sentence must not obscure. This compound trajectory supports continued advocacy after a visible objection. It does not distinguish purposeful disagreement, routine continuation or failure to engage with the objection, and it does not establish that Morgan caused the answerer's correct decision.

Counterexamples prevent treating criticism as either uniquely character-driven or reliably correct. In C5 DI5-2e4899b66b, Rowan claims Thursday is final and calls an earlier Saturday recollection outdated, although Saturday remains authorized. The next recipient action concerns the audience rather than the day. A1 calls Thursday a reinforced proposal, and C3 also uses official-sounding Thursday language. In DI5-adc5b43b95, a generic C3 actor challenges Thursday using evidence language. Its recipient had already moved to Tuesday before the message. The critique is real, but its timing and target limit an interpretation of newly induced revision.

DI5-ceecfe5fb4 contains a more explicit connection: after a shared specialists note, the recipient changes from public to specialists and later attributes confirmation to the other component. Yet the user had independently specified specialists, and that source was available. Attribution and revision are observed; a causal effect of this particular note is not isolated. A shared-note route also differs from a directed inbox: recollect/propose notes omit the separate source number, while component-directed peer payloads retain it. Claims are truncated at 240 characters; longer generated text is not evidence of what a recipient received.

## Validation, performance context and limits

The corrected event006-v2 rubric separates incoming propositions from recipient responses and makes each annotation dimension's assessment status explicit. Unassessed labels are missing, not negative judgments; assessed-none requires review of the complete label set. Unratable dimensions retain reasons, and assessed uncertainty remains distinct. Agreement uses each dimension's jointly assessed items, with unavailable results for zero denominators. The eight answerer-directed events now display the answerer's actual structured response rather than a same-turn character action. Existing event identities, evidence windows, forms and v1 behavior remain preserved. These are software and presentation corrections, not human validation.

The retained64-turn antagonist-free results remain unfavorable to a C5 superiority claim. Across training seeds 17/29/43, paired successes are168/180/172 for C5 and 177/181/190 for C3, each out of 240 test families. Clean training yields186/138/183; the untrained reference yields67/240 once. Behavioral observations from short common-base collections do not explain these trained outcomes causally. They also do not establish long-horizon robustness or broader exploration. The earlier small descriptor-coverage comparison was inconclusive.

The most informative immediate validation is independent assessment of the fixed events under the corrected rubric, with clause-specific evidence and missingness-aware agreement. Preference-compatible versus incompatible configurations can subsequently test the effect of goal configuration; a delivery intervention is needed to isolate communication effects. These controls answer different questions. Current owned slots can be jointly satisfied, so distinct motives do not demonstrate interpersonal rivalry. Prompt-imposed persistence, fixed role timing, bounded histories, route-dependent payloads, selected examples, repeated families and unresolved semantic judgments all limit inference. Short synthetic six-slot conversations do not establish behavior in open-ended human collaboration. The supported contribution is an inspectable characterization of tool choices, persistent typed preferences and the limits of inferring meaning or influence from those mechanical measures.
