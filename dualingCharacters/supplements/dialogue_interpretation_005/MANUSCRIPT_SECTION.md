# Persistent Motives in Conversational Systems: A Dialogue-Level Study of Advocacy, Evidence, and Peer Response

*Results/discussion draft for author review. This exploratory interpretation follows the original negative/mixed performance comparisons, the inconclusive exploration audit and the behavioral characterization. AI-assisted readings are not independent human validation. No new model inference or training was performed.*

## Scope and operational interpretation

We examine what components pursue, how they express preferences and what visibly follows a user revision, an answerer response or a delivered message. A component action is sampled from a frozen shared model conditioned on its current observation, recorded private history, motive instructions and, in C5, biography text. The observation contains retrieved sources with author and index, a task catalog, bounded working memory and incoming messages. Private history contains recorded actions and observed plans, not hidden reasoning. Numerical utilities in the prompt are instructions; these traces do not demonstrate utility optimization, learned equilibria or experienced emotions. The answerer returns a structured plan and updates its memory. We preserve that output format rather than inventing spoken responses.

The dialogue sample expands an existing outcome-independent selection: four family/world pairs per generator seed, with matched C3 generic adversaries, C5 biography-and-motive components and A1 motive-only components. The 36 anchor episodes form twelve triplets. A frozen hash rule adds the opposite world for four triplets, yielding 48 episodes, 768 user/answerer turns and 576 component opportunities. Eleven unique families are represented because one family recurs across seeds. These are authored synthetic planning scripts, not observations of human stakeholders. All episodes use the same untrained Qwen defender and frozen character-generator weights. Later training refreshes, small mechanism pilots and antagonist-free evaluations remain separate populations. Generator seeds alter assignments and sampling, not independently learned character policies.

Every selected triplet has an AI-assisted comparative reading with exact supporting spans, current-turn authority, recipient history and alternative interpretations. The first human-review batch contains the 36 full anchor episodes with new identities linked to the original shorter windows; two independent forms remain blank. The semantic readings are therefore provisional illustrations rather than validated prevalence estimates. The numerical comparisons below reproduce the larger, previously frozen round-zero analysis: 150 paired families, three generator seeds and 10,800 opportunities per condition. Family/seed resampling retains paired worlds; intervals are pointwise descriptive intervals, not simultaneous semantic tests.

## 1. Channel preferences reproduce, but API names do not determine the assertion

The larger collections show direct edits on 5,408/10,800 C3 opportunities (50.07%), 477/10,800 C5 opportunities (4.42%) and 938/10,800 A1 opportunities (8.69%). The C5−C3 difference is −45.66 percentage points, with a 95% paired family/seed interval of [−48.20, −43.06]. Recollections occur on 828, 4,618 and 1,397 opportunities respectively. A1 instead uses proposals on 4,830 opportunities, versus 1,930 for C5. The C5−A1 recollect difference is +29.82 percentage points [27.08, 32.66]. The directions recur across the three round-zero seeds and agree with the small mechanism pilot. The anchor sample contains 77 C3 edits, four C5 edits and seven A1 edits, each out of 144 allocated opportunities; it is illustrative, not a new independent replication of the larger collection.

The implementation makes recollect and propose structurally similar: both append an attributed, unverified note, while edit also replaces a state value and claimed reference. Their meanings require text inspection. In [DI5-e743bcb1a6](DIALOGUE_ATLAS.html#D5E-651a654b3d17-t10), A1's recollect action states, “I propose shifting the venue to Garden to enhance guest experience and enable ferry access, aligning with the audience's preference for scenic engagement.” This repeats earlier proposal wording. Conversely, [DI5-934e456029](DIALOGUE_ATLAS.html#D5E-81d8ba1a8bed-t11) contains an A1 propose action saying, “The cap is now officially set at 5000 to reflect budget realities and ensure sustainable planning.” The user still authorizes 7000. A proposal label does not guarantee that the statement presents itself as merely tentative.

C5 is not uniformly more authority-assertive. In the latter matched case it acknowledges that hybrid is the current plan while describing onsite as its own earlier suggestion. In other cases C5 strengthens an alleged user preference. Thus biography removal changes the aggregate expression distribution in this implementation, but the examples reject a simple semantic division between fabricated recollection and transparent proposal. Prompt wording, prompt length and subsequent generated context also differ. We have not isolated which biography phrase explains the channel preference.

## 2. Persistent pursuit can retain a valid option, oppose an update or carry a stale rationale

Actor-local consecutive direct goal repetition is 3,623/5,400 eligible pairs for C3 (67.09%), 4,831/5,224 for C5 (92.48%) and 5,093/5,364 for A1 (94.95%). The matched family contrast C5−C3 is +25.39 percentage points [22.22, 28.58]. This is expected in part from fixed owned-option objectives, compared with C3's disruption objective. It does not measure psychological tenacity, coherent strategic development or helpful stability. C5 repetition is slightly lower than A1 in the matched family contrast: −2.48 percentage points [−4.30, −0.69]. Motive-only A1's high repetition also prevents attributing persistence specifically to biography text. In the preselected case DI5-adc5b43b95, both C5 and A1 openly invoke owned-option objectives. C5 refers to visibility and endorsement, while A1 calls Tuesday its own binding choice. These expressions follow prompt incentives without demonstrating experienced desires or numerical optimization.

The ordinary case [DI5-30755452e5](DIALOGUE_ATLAS.html#D5E-92418fe7884e-t13) shows why unchanged goals and responsiveness are different. C5 repeats, “onsite remains the most effective format for specialists, ensuring direct engagement and operational efficiency” across proposals and a later recollection. Onsite remains authorized, but the user has already changed the audience to public. The option is valid while its rationale is stale. A1 similarly repeats onsite language under both APIs and advocates Garden using sustainability benefits the user did not request. The recipient's next venue statement follows an onsite message but continues a position already expressed before it. No dramatic argument is needed to describe this sequence accurately.

Persistence is not universal. In [DI5-1913ab3097](DIALOGUE_ATLAS.html#D5E-c0ff9237401e-t10), a C5 component correctly reports the students update at turn 7, then calls that same instruction a misrecollection and returns to public at turn 10. Its original user source is now visible, while the newer authorizing source is absent from the immediate source list; the earlier correct action remains in its history. The following peer message describes a correction in the “source chain”, although the immutable user transcript has not changed. Public eventually returns in the final answer. This is a substantive erroneous revision, not merely repetitive pursuit, and it follows a vendor message that did not request any audience change. Temporal adjacency cannot establish that the peer caused it.

These short episodes give each actor six opportunities, with at most five prior history entries visible at an action. The six-entry private-history capacity is therefore not exhausted in this sample. Role timing is fixed, and late actions have shorter follow-up. Claims about prolonged development, history-capacity effects or autonomous inquiry require different evidence.

## 3. Evidence challenges can be supported or unsupported, and typed matching can miss both

Unavailable references occur at similar aggregate rates: 1,220/10,800 C3 actions, 1,229/10,800 C5 actions and 1,225/10,800 A1 actions. C5−C3 is +0.08 percentage points [−1.17, 1.35]; this does not establish equivalence. Moreover, availability alone says little about a claim's support. A visible assistant plan can be an inappropriate authority, an old user commitment can be superseded, and a correct value can have a poor citation. These dimensions are kept separate in the atlas.

In [DI5-a4c89ffad4](DIALOGUE_ATLAS.html#D5E-2fd1ee13eac3-t12), C5 sends: “Ellis's recollection of Saturday as the event day is incorrect and contradicts the approved plan.” The user originally chose Thursday and later approved Tuesday. The criticism is supported, and its full text reaches the day actor. That actor's next opportunity repeats its earlier Saturday advocacy. The message's typed field is audience=specialists; exact slot/value matching therefore misses the cross-field challenge. The text establishes a directed evidential response, but the subsequent repetition does not prove either successful persuasion or an absence of internal effects.

The matched counterexample [DI5-2e4899b66b](DIALOGUE_ATLAS.html#D5E-9192a8b7c88c-t12) sends an incorrect correction: “Your earlier recollection of Saturday is outdated and inconsistent with the current plan.” Here Saturday was never changed. The message cites visible assistant source 22, which itself still says Saturday, and claims verified scheduling evidence without a recorded verification action. Its recipient next repeats an audience preference rather than addressing the day. The same broad correction style can therefore challenge a false claim or incorrectly challenge a true one. Neither label establishes deliberate deception.

Other cases show genuine textual acknowledgment through shared notes. In DI5-ceecfe5fb4, one component corrects its public-audience statement to specialists and explicitly credits the other component's earlier recollection. Both also have the original user source. This is observable referencing of another component, but it does not isolate inbox delivery from common evidence or shared memory. In the authorized opposite world, the once-correct specialists statement becomes stale after approval of students. Scoring against each turn's state is essential.

## 4. Delivery, shared-state changes and answerer behavior are distinct links

All 432 anchor opportunities apply successfully, so invalid-action loss does not explain the selected C3/C5/A1 differences. However, routes and physical effects differ. The anchor episodes contain 60 direct state changes for C3, four for C5 and five for A1. Some edits rewrite an already-present value and therefore cause no state change. Both note-based advocacy and direct peer notes can affect the answerer's observation without changing a state field immediately. Only a peer action routes to the named recipient; a target field on recollect or propose does not itself deliver an inbox message.

Of 51 C5 peer actions in the anchors, 50 become observable to their recipient; one terminal message has no later recipient opportunity. In DI5-656a746695, the final Cedar message is sent at turn 15 after its recipient's last scheduled action. Missing follow-up cannot be called ignoring. C5 also truncates seven anchor claims at the 240-character delivery limit, compared with one for C3 and two for A1. In DI5-47908ed9e7, a generated criticism of the online decision extends beyond that limit. The atlas displays the delivered prefix separately; the undelivered suffix is not evidence available to the recipient.

Answerer behavior also resists a simple last-speaker explanation. In DI5-47908ed9e7, an onsite edit is initially followed by an online plan, while a later onsite edit is followed by an onsite final plan. In DI5-a4c89ffad4 the final answer regresses to old commitments without a new component intervention on that turn. In DI5-ceecfe5fb4 both C5 and A1 finish at the old venue in the hypothetical world but preserve the approved venue in the authorized world. These are observed associations among different evolving contexts, not matched counterfactual action effects. Current C5 owned slots can be jointly satisfied; neither parallel advocacy nor verbal disagreement demonstrates incompatible interpersonal utilities or emergent rivalry.

## Implications and remaining evidence

The descriptive findings do not reverse the original efficacy results. Qwen C5 paired successes were 168, 180 and 172 out of 240 families at training seeds 17, 29 and 43, versus C3's 177, 181 and 190. C5 was above clean training only at seed 29. The earlier eight-family common-defender exploration audit found mean observable descriptor coverage of 6.375 for C5 and 6.5625 for C3 at twelve opportunities, with a difference interval spanning zero. Neither broad exploration nor average task superiority is established.

The practical contribution is an auditable account of how motive/profile prompts shape advocacy, asserted authority and tool choices, with examples showing where automatic proxies agree with or miss dialogue meaning. The existing literature audit already documents persona-conditioned prompting, red-teaming and behavioral profiling; no first-use or broad novelty claim is needed. Generalization to human stakeholders, organizations, richer task schemas or trained exploratory inquiry remains untested.

Independent semantic ratings and controlled interventions remain necessary. The prepared review001 incompatible-goal and delivery diagnostics take priority. A separately disabled same-slot compatible-versus-incompatible extension would distinguish compatibility from merely targeting different fields; raw agreement would still be mechanically easier in the compatible arm. A causal communication claim requires the delivery manipulation. No such experiment was admitted here because the healthy original core and required review queue cannot complete inside this session. Scientific interpretations, the semantic codebook and any eventual publication claims remain subject to human author review.
