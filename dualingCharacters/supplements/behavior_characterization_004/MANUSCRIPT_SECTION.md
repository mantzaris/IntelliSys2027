# Author-review results and discussion

## Results: observable behavior under persistent motive conditioning

We characterized completed training interactions after the original mixed/negative accuracy results and an inconclusive exploration audit. The main behavioral comparison held the untrained Qwen defender, family/world pairs, 16-turn horizon, 12-opportunity schedule, tools and generator weights fixed, using 150 unique families across three generator seeds. Refreshed collections and the eight-family mechanism pilot were analyzed separately. This was an exploratory descriptive analysis, not an original confirmatory endpoint.

Character-conditioned generators used fewer direct edits and more recollection actions than generic adversaries. Edits occurred on 5,408/10,800 (50.07%) C3 and 477/10,800 (4.42%) C5 opportunities, a matched-family difference of -45.66 pp [95% interval -48.20, -43.06]. Recollections showed the opposite difference, +35.09 pp [95% interval +31.64, +38.67]. Removing biography text while preserving explicit motives shifted actions toward proposals: C5−A1 recollection +29.82 pp [95% interval +27.08, +32.66], proposal -26.85 pp [95% interval -29.29, -24.40]. All three seeds reproduced these directions. Observable shared-state writes also differed, but notes, direct edits and subsequent defender outputs were treated as separate channels.

Within-actor consecutive typed-goal repetition was 3,623/5,400 (67.09%) for C3, 4,831/5,224 (92.48%) for C5 and 5,093/5,364 (94.95%) for A1. The fixed-goal conditions often continued their owned option after authorized user revisions away from it. This is consistent with the supplied utilities, not evidence of helpful robustness. API changes and exact text changes were reported separately; neither establishes substantive strategy development.

Unavailable-reference rates were similar descriptively, with C5−C3 +0.08 pp [95% interval -1.17, +1.35], without an equivalence claim. Current support, superseded authority, assistant sources and canonical mismatch were distinguished. Delivered peer messages were usually observed, but C5 next-opportunity typed matches were only 39/3,044 (1.28%). The recipient's prior behavior provides a descriptive reference, not a counterfactual. Deterministic text examples show that typed matching can miss a corrective disclosure and can also match an already pursued goal. Human semantic validation is pending.

## Discussion and limits

The most defensible contribution is a reproducible characterization of how this motive-conditioned system acts: channel selection, persistent goal endorsement, evidence relations and limited measured peer response. The biography ablation affects channel use despite similar descriptor coverage in the prior audit. This isolates removal of these biography strings, including their length and wording, rather than narrative sophistication in general. Private history and communication are not separately established mechanisms: their larger main cells are pending, and the pilot communication ablation incurs rejected actions. Fixed role timing and jointly satisfiable owned slots constrain any interpersonal explanation.

Behavioral differences do not imply superiority. C5 paired successes at seeds 17/29/43 are 168/180/172 of 240, compared with C3's 177/181/190 and clean C1's 186/138/183. Thus C5 remains below C3 in every available seed; the clean comparison is mixed. The existing exploration audit remains inconclusive. Different interventions may alter recovery diversity and supervised exposure, but there is no causal mediation analysis or matched-severity counterfactual here. Independent-model learned replication, naturalistic intent evaluation and human ratings remain separate pending evidence.

Profiles and utility terms are prompts for frozen generators; retrieval is fixed, while the answerer receives supervised adapter updates in the original campaign. We make no claim of learned character policies, co-evolution, psychological personality, emergent coalitions, equilibrium optimization or open-ended planning. Interpretations and scientific arguments require human author review. See RELATED_WORK.md for primary-method precedents and NEXT_EXPERIMENT.md for the already prepared discriminating diagnostic.
